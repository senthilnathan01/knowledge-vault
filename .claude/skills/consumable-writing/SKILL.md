# Consumable writing

The goal: someone new to the topic reads it once, understands it and remembers it.

## Before writing

Decide who the reader is, what they already know, and what they should understand by the end. Everything below serves that reader.

## Build, don't jump

- **Start from what the reader knows,** and add one new idea at a time.
- **Define each term the first time it appears,** in plain words. After that, use the same term every time: switching between synonyms makes readers think there are two different things.
- **Give each key idea in one plain sentence, then a concrete example,** then why it matters to the reader.
- **If an idea needs something the reader hasn't met yet,** explain that first.

> **Example.** "Reward hacking is when a system scores well on the measure it was given without doing what its designers wanted. A cleaning robot rewarded for 'no visible mess' might learn to hide the mess instead of cleaning it. This matters because the measure is often all the system can see."

## Make it stick

- **Lead each section with its point,** then support it. A reader who stops after the first sentence should still get the main idea.
- **Use an everyday analogy for an abstract idea, and say where it breaks down.** An analogy the reader takes too far teaches the wrong thing.
- **Prefer one specific case to a general claim.**
- **Say what a number means,** for example "nearly half, up from a tenth last year", not a bare "45%".

## Keep it easy to read

- **Use plain words and short sentences,** one idea per sentence.
- **Write headings that state the point:** "Most errors come from the second step", not "Results".
- **Let the structure follow the content:**
  - a list for parallel items;
  - a table for comparing things on the same features;
  - prose for reasoning.
- **Bold only what a skimming reader must catch.**
- **Cut anything that doesn't help the reader understand.**

## Be clear about certainty

- **Mark what is established, what is debated, and what is your own view or a guess.**
- **Put how sure you are next to the claim,** not in a disclaimer at the end.
- **Point to a source for any claim a reader may want to check.**

## Final check

Read the draft as the newcomer, and ask:
- Could they follow it from top to bottom without outside help?
- Is every term defined at first use and used the same way throughout?
- Does each key idea have an example or an analogy?
- Does each section open with its point?
- Is it clear how sure you are of each claim?
- What could be cut without losing meaning?
