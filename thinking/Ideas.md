## Open

### Model the race between AI labs as a game

- From: Pachocki saying OpenAI aims at recursive self-improvement as "the only way to remain at the frontier" ([[Jakub Pachocki - An Alien Mind]]), and my [[Game Theory and Strategic Interaction]] project. Lesson 1.3 of [[BlueDot AGI Strategy Course]] covers the same dynamic.
- The idea: write each lab's choice to go careful or go fast as a payoff matrix (a table of what each side gets for each combination of choices). Check whether it is a prisoner's dilemma: each lab does better by racing whatever the other does, yet both racing is worse for both than both being careful. Then see which escape routes game theory predicts (binding agreements, verification, changing the payoffs) and compare them with what the course readings propose.
- Why it matters: if the race is a prisoner's dilemma, no lab can afford to slow down alone. That would explain why Pachocki asks for mandated safety bars rather than one lab's restraint.
- First step: after lesson 1.3, draft a two-lab matrix in [[Game Theory and AI Safety]], the note the game theory project already plans.

### Give an AI coding agent one checkable task and one judgment-heavy task, and compare where each breaks

- From: BlueDot lesson 2.2, which suggests pushing models to their limits with Claude Code or Codex, for example on a task I dislike doing.
- The idea: pick one task with a clear test of success (a script that must pass checks) and one that needs judgment (a short plan or design), let an agent work on each with little help, and note where each succeeds and fails.
- Why it matters: it gives first-hand evidence for the hill-climbable versus judgment-heavy split in [[Measuring AI Capability]], instead of relying only on reports.
- First step: choose the two tasks and record what happened as a short note here.

### Make forecasts on Metaculus's AI 2027 questions

- From: BlueDot lesson 2.2 (optional practice). The series tests claims from the AI 2027 scenario (Kokotajlo et al., 2025): AI automating AI research by early 2027, a US-China race, and later signs of misalignment.[^metaculus-ai2027]
- Why it matters: putting numbers on my own expectations, then checking them later, is a direct way to test my views in [[AI Timelines]].
- First step: forecast three questions and log the numbers and reasons here.
