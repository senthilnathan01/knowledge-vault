# Thinking

This folder holds my own questions, ideas and the conclusions I reach in discussion. Reading and ingesting tell me what other people think. This is where I work out what I think, so it matters more than any single source note.

## Each kind of thought has its own place

| What | Where | When it moves on |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A question I want answered | [[Open Questions]], one entry each | When it gets real thought, it becomes its own note in this folder |
| An idea: something to try, build, write or connect | [[Ideas]], one entry each | When I start on it, it becomes a project in `projects/` or its own note here |
| A question or idea I have thought through, usually in a discussion | Its own note in this folder, from `templates/Thinking Note.md` | When the answer is settled knowledge, the conclusion goes into a concept note and links back here |

## An entry is cheap, a thinking note is earned

Adding to the lists should take a minute: the question, where it came from, and why it matters. Don't wait until it's well formed.

A thinking note is for when a question has had real work: a discussion, some reading, a first answer. It records what I think now, how sure I am, the best arguments on each side, and what would change my mind. That last part is what makes it thinking rather than a summary.

## Claude's suggestions stay marked until I've discussed them

An entry may carry "Claude's first take:" until we discuss it. After that it becomes "My take:", or moves into its own thinking note. Status is one of open, thinking or settled. Anything else, such as waiting on a lesson, goes in the entry's "Next step" line.

The same rule applies to source notes: an interpretation section written during an ingest is labelled as Claude's reading until I've discussed it.

## Where entries come from

- Discussions with Claude. When a discussion turns up a question or a conclusion, it goes here before the conversation ends.
- Ingesting a source. Each ingest ends with a few questions worth discussing, and they are added to [[Open Questions]] with a link to the source note.
- Course exercises and prompts, such as the BlueDot course asking which step of a reading's argument I would investigate before deciding whether I agree.
- Anything else that occurs to me.
